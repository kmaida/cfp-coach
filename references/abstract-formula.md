# Titles and abstract seeds

Read the active profile's title rules and relevant submission history first. Keep established canonical titles when the profile requires them. Offer provisional alternatives only for new talks or when explicitly requested.

Choose a structure that fits the material: concrete capability followed by a gap; a real incident followed by its lesson; a defensible number; a specific contrarian claim; or a sharp question. Vary structures across seeds. Never invent incidents or statistics.

Use a concrete problem, evidence the speaker owns, and attendee outcomes. Prefer standards or transferable patterns over a product pitch unless the speaker's profile and the target CFP permit one. Domain examples come from the active speaker, never another profile.

Titles should be specific, readable aloud, and accurate about the promised content. Avoid generic "A Journey", "Beyond X", "The Future of X", "Unlocking", and "Demystifying" framing unless the material earns it. Profile title patterns override these defaults. Conference title statistics are dated calibration, not universal rules.

Default seed length: 80-150 words, subject to the form and profile. Include actionable attendee takeaways and `[NEEDS: ...]` for missing evidence. Mark new terminology as a proposed coinage and explain the credibility risk.
