# CFP review rubric

Grade drafts against these criteria. An issue is blocking if it plausibly costs the acceptance at the target conference.

## 1. Reviewer criteria fit

If the conference's CFP guide is in the bank or retrievable, check the draft against its stated criteria line by line. Committees score against their own rubric first; a beautiful abstract that ignores the stated audience or format loses to a plain one that nails them. Missing stated criteria: blocking.

## 2. First-line hook

Reviewers skim hundreds of abstracts. The first sentence must establish a concrete problem or tension. Openings that describe the field's general state ("AI agents are transforming...") are blocking. Openings that name a specific failure, number, or moment pass.

## 3. Specificity

Count the concrete nouns: named protocols, real numbers, actual failure modes, specific tools. An abstract a competitor could have written about their own talk is too generic. Fewer than three concrete specifics: blocking.

## 4. Takeaways

- Phrased as attendee outcomes, not speaker topics ("you'll leave able to X" not "we will cover X").
- At least one usable Monday morning.
- Match the abstract's promises. A takeaway the abstract never sets up: suggestion-level. A takeaway the talk can't deliver: blocking.

## 5. Vendor smell

Any product name, product-shaped feature description, or "our platform" phrasing in the abstract: blocking. The pivot must land on a standard or pattern. Apply the active profile's employer and product constraints; check the bio separately. Profile rules override this generic vendor default.

## 6. Overclaim audit

Flag every claim that would need a citation or demo to survive Q&A. Require evidence the active speaker can defend on stage. Superlatives ("first," "only," "solves") get special scrutiny. Undefendable claim: blocking.

## 7. Saturation and differentiation

Compare against the bank and recent schedules for the target conference. If the angle matches 3+ recent accepted talks with no stated twist, blocking. If it fills a documented gap, say so in the competitive read; that's a selling point the speaker can use.

## 8. Audience calibration

Does the difficulty level match the conference? A 101 talk at a practitioner conference and a deep protocol talk at a business conference both lose. Check the CFP guide's audience statement and prior schedules' level mix.

## 9. Voice and writing checks

Apply the entire active profile's voice guide and required writing dependencies; otherwise use the self-contained writing defaults in SKILL.md. Isolated violations: nitpicks. Pervasive violations (reads AI-generated): blocking, because committees increasingly reject abstracts that smell generated.

## 10. Title

- Canonical title consistency with the active speaker's prior submissions and title policy.
- Passes the read-aloud test: one breath, no parser stall.
- No tired shapes (see abstract-formula.md).
- Title promises what the abstract delivers. Mismatch: suggestion-level unless severe.

## 11. Bio (when included)

Third person unless the form says otherwise, credibility specific to this talk's topic, employer named according to the profile, no adjectives doing credential work ("passionate," "seasoned"). Length per form limits.

## 12. Logistics

Check form constraints when known: character limits, required fields (level, format, track), video requirements. An abstract 40% over the character limit is blocking because the cut will be rushed otherwise.
